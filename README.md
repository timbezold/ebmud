# EBMUD Watershed Regulatory Enforcement & Illegal Dumping Analysis Report

**Document Reference:** PRR-2026-227  
**Agency Reviewed:** East Bay Municipal Utility District (EBMUD)  
**Primary Focus Area:** South Watershed (Pinehurst Road / Upper San Leandro Reservoir Corridor)  
**Data Evaluation Period:** Citation Data (May 2025 – August 2026) | Dumping Data (January 2023 – August 2026)  

---

## 1. Executive Summary

An evaluation of public records provided by the East Bay Municipal Utility District (EBMUD) under Request PRR-2026-227 reveals a substantial operational gap between administrative checks on recreational trail users and active deterrence of illegal dumping on watershed lands. 

While EBMUD issued **196 total regulatory citations** across district properties from May 2025 through August 2026, **187 (95.4%)** were issued to hikers, runners, and cyclists for lacking a valid $10 trail permit. In contrast, EBMUD logged only **9 total illegal dumping incidents** across its entire jurisdiction over a 3.5-year period (2023–2026).

Along the sensitive **South Watershed corridor** (Pinehurst Road / Upper San Leandro Reservoir), rangers issued **102 trail permit citations** while logging only **4 illegal dumping incidents** during the same 15-month citation timeframe—an enforcement ratio of **25.5 permit citations for every 1 recorded dumping incident**.

## 3. Analysis of Contrasting Priorities

### A. High-Volume Administrative Checks vs. Environmental Defense
The public records demonstrate that field ranger patrols prioritize low-friction administrative checks on recreational users over perimeter security against environmental crimes. Stopping trail users during daylight hours at designated trailheads yields high enforcement volume with minimal effort, but produces virtually no water-quality or environmental protection benefits.

### B. Spatial Concentration in the South Watershed
More than half of all citations issued across EBMUD’s multi-county service area occurred along the South Watershed corridor. Out of 103 total citations in this corridor, 102 were handed to hikers without permits, and 1 was issued for a fish and game violation. This geographic concentration demonstrates that ranger hours are heavily concentrated on recreational access corridors rather than unmonitored road pullouts prone to hazardous waste dumping.

### C. Remediation Model vs. Active Enforcement
EBMUD logged only 9 illegal dumping incidents across 42 months. This low tracking figure suggests that EBMUD treats roadside dumping primarily as an offline cleanup/maintenance responsibility rather than a criminal enforcement priority. While dumping introduces toxic materials (paints, oils, batteries, construction waste) directly into soil feeding the Upper San Leandro Reservoir, field resources remain focused on validating paper and mobile trail permits.

---

## 4. Strategic Governance Recommendations

1. **Automate Trail Permit Compliance:** Transition trail permitting entirely to digital, self-service mobile platforms with passive QR-code verification at trailheads. Automating compliance checks frees ranger field hours for physical perimeter security and watershed monitoring.
2. **Deploy Solar-Powered Automated License-Plate Readers (ALPR):** Install motion-activated, night-vision cameras at known dumping pullouts along Pinehurst Road. Automated surveillance provides continuous deterrence without requiring constant physical ranger presence.
3. **Reallocate Night and Perimeter Patrol Hours:** Shift a portion of daytime ranger patrol hours toward late-evening and early-morning perimeter sweeps along road corridors vulnerable to waste abandonment.
4. **Formalize Joint Law Enforcement Protocols:** Establish joint task-force agreements with the Contra Costa County Sheriff’s Office and County Public Works to streamline criminal prosecution and rapid inter-agency referrals for illegal dumping.
